02 / What people will see

A desirable store.
An understandable business.

The landing previews both experiences and the photos-to-store transformation. Once inside, Try the dashboard and View storefront make switching obvious.

A fashion collection worth browsing.

  • Strong imagery, alternate views, zoom and quick view.
  • Useful filters and category-appropriate size options.
  • A shopping assistant that uses actual catalogue products.
  • A clear bag, complete sample totals and demo checkout.

Example shopping moment

“Build a look under $150, including delivery.”

A $54 shirt, $68 trousers and $22 belt make $144 in merchandise. With $6 demo delivery, the reviewed total is $150.

The visitor can swap a piece and approve the bag. The assistant does not buy anything automatically.

Useful operations behind the collection.

  • Sales, orders, average order value and fulfilment attention.
  • Charts that open their supporting records and formulas.
  • Separate returns, refunds and stock movements.
  • Synthetic feedback and an assistant that explains the evidence.

03 / Where AI earns its place

Three useful roles.
Clear human control.

OpenAI is the intended provider. The app checks the result and calculates the business facts; the visitor controls changes and confirmations.

Create

Catalogue agent

Groups supplied photos and drafts product titles, categories and grounded copy. Unknown facts stay unknown.

Visitor reviews the draft and opens the store.

Shop

Shopping assistant

Compares products, suggests looks within budget and prepares a bag using current options and demo stock.

Visitor approves additions and the demo order.

Operate

Owner assistant

Explains current figures, opens supporting records and prepares improved catalogue copy from known facts.

Visitor reviews edits in a real form before applying.

Honest by design: photographs cannot prove materials, measurements, stock or past sales. Prepared sample interpretation is labelled; live image analysis is explicit. All commerce history is synthetic.

04 / The first build

A complete demo,
with a deliberate scope.

The goal is to show clients a reusable ecommerce foundation and a coherent AI experience. Production integrations are separately scoped.

In this demo

  • Landing, sample/upload intake and reviewable catalogue.
  • Polished storefront, shopping help and simulated checkout.
  • Connected dashboard, orders, inventory, returns and refunds.
  • Inspectable figures, synthetic feedback and owner assistance.
  • Temporary guest recovery, JSON export and reset.

Separately scoped later

  • Payment processing, real refunds and shipping/carrier APIs.
  • Shopify or other commerce-platform integrations.
  • Merchant accounts, organizations and persistent business data.
  • Public store publishing, custom store domains and subscriptions.
  • Virtual try-on, generated worn photos and advanced site building.

05 / The delivery foundation

The same proven
demo backbone.

Inspired by direct exploration of ClipBuilder, ScreenPilot and Retrosum, with the framework, repository approach and deployment tool family carried forward.

ClipBuilderScreenPilotRetrosum

One frontend. One private backend. Shared rules. The store and dashboard use the same exact commerce engine. AI processing stays behind the private API; provider keys stay off the browser.

The guest workspace has a fixed two-hour lifetime. There is no hosted business database or upload library in the first demo. Approved live processing sends selected image previews and relevant context to the provider.

Development starts with deterministic fakes and paid AI disabled. Live use is deliberately enabled, bounded and measured.

Show the planned technology and folder shape
  • Framework: Node, pnpm, strict TypeScript, Next.js/React, Express and Zod.
  • Repository: apps/frontend, apps/backend, packages/contracts and packages/commerce; focused docs, scripts and tests.
  • Hosting: Docker, Google Cloud Run, Artifact Registry and Secret Manager; Cloudflare for chosen domain/challenge setup.
  • Release: unpaid quality checks and an owner-controlled immutable frontend/backend image pair.
